u u — Privacy Policy

Last updated 3 September 2026. This is the same text the app shows, at the consent gate and again under Settings → What happens to my photos. It is written from what the code does, not from what a policy usually says.

The short version

u u has no accounts, no analytics, no advertising and no trackers. Your photos, your likeness and every picture u u makes are stored on your iPhone. They leave it only to be turned into pictures, and nothing keeps them at the other end. Photographs of your own clothes, in your closet, do not leave it at all.

Your photos

You choose which photos to give u u. Apple's picker hands over only the ones you select — u u never gets access to your library. They are sent once, to make your likeness, and are not stored on any server. The likeness itself lives on your iPhone. After that your original photos are not sent anywhere again: every look is made from the likeness.

Your closet

Photographs of your own clothes are different from the photographs of you, and the difference is worth stating plainly: they never leave your iPhone. You photograph a garment, or choose a picture of one. iOS cuts it out from its background on the device — the same mechanism that lifts a subject out of a photo in Apple's Photos app — and the cut-out is saved on your iPhone. Nothing about a closet piece is sent to u u, to the image service, or to any shop. Not the photograph, not the cut-out, not what you called it, not how you scored it. What it changes is what u u searches for next, and that happens on your iPhone too.

Your likeness

A likeness is a picture of you and, in Illinois, Texas and Washington, a biometric identifier. u u asks you to agree before it makes one. It is stored on your iPhone, sent to the image service each time a look is made, and deleted the moment you ask.

Who processes what

The image service. Your likeness and the shop's product photo are sent to OpenRouter, which runs Meta's Muse image model, to make each picture. Their handling is governed by their own terms. u u's server. A small Cloudflare Worker holds the image-service key so the app never has to. It passes images straight through and keeps none of them. It records only how many images were in a request, how long the prompt was, what the image cost and how long it took — no pictures, no prompt text, nothing that identifies you. Cloudflare sees the network address the request came from, as any web server does, and it is used to stop one device making unlimited images. Shopify. Searching for clothes sends the search words and a country to Shopify's catalog. Building a bag sends the items to that shop. Neither carries your name, your likeness or an account. The shops. Loading a product photo is a request to that shop's image server, as loading any web image is. Buying happens on their site under their terms.

What u u never does

  • No account, no email, no phone number
  • No analytics, crash reporting or advertising identifiers
  • No selling or sharing of anything with anyone for advertising
  • No payment details — u u never takes payment
  • No sending your photos to anyone except to make your pictures

Deleting

Settings → Delete my photos and likeness removes your likeness, every look, every close-up, every piece you photographed into your closet, and every record of them from your iPhone, and withdraws your agreement. It cannot be undone, and there is no copy elsewhere to ask for.

Children

u u is intended for people aged 12 and over. If you are under the age of majority where you live, use u u only with permission from a parent or legal guardian. u u does not knowingly build a likeness of a child under 12.
